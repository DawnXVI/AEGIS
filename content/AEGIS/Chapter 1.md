### Loria, 20XX

The sun crests the horizon, its amber glow washing over the land and bathing it in pleasant warmth. The Kingdom of Loria awakens beneath the radiance, its cities quickly filling with noise as denizens spill onto the streets. Vendors call out their wares. Blacksmiths' hammers ring against anvils. Tavern doors swing open to welcome the first customers of the day.

The sunlight shines most elegantly upon Loria's capital, Edenburth.

A tall, heavily guarded fence frames the perimeter of the city's center. Just past the gates, a long stretch of land filled with lush greenery sprawls outward. Atop a steep hill stands the city's most imposing structure, a castle that has remained unscathed for centuries.

The castle of **King Rodin.**

Someone knocks on the large doors to the grand hall. A long, purple carpet runs along the floor beyond them, leading up to a short flight of stairs. Atop sit two thrones, crafted from gold and the finest marble available.

His Majesty occupies the right throne. The left remains conspicuously empty.

"Enter."

His authoritative voice booms throughout the hall without him even trying to be loud. The doors swing open, revealing a woman with short auburn hair and feline ears atop her head. She walks inside, a two-handed greatsword mounted on her back.

The torches adorning the hall crackle faintly as she makes her way along the carpet. Her ears twitch with each audible snap and pop from the flames.

Once she reaches the base of the staircase, she drops to one knee, lowering her head in reverence.

"Your Majesty. The expedition team has set off to Gumera Cavern, as requested."

Rodin leans back slightly, nodding in approval.

"Good. And what of the search?"

The woman's stoicism falters for just a second. Unease creeps down her spine before she forces herself steady.

"...Still nothing, sire," she admits, her voice barely holding.

The King's expression doesn't change. And yet, the temperature in the room seems to plummet—several tens of degrees in an instant.

"Why is it that my army's _best_ soldiers haven't been able to locate **one** lone demon? What more resources must I give you before you bring me _**results?**_"

The woman makes no move to raise her head. Her gaze stays fixed on the floor.

"My deepest apologies, Your Majesty… We just... don't have any good leads. The only conclusion we've reached is that _someone_ is harboring the demon, but we've had no luck narrowing down suspects."

"Then be more **direct.**" Rodin snaps back, his stare burning a hole through her head. "Have inspectors visit homes. Interrogate civilians, if you must."

He crosses his arms, letting out a long, _frustrated_ breath and closing his eyes. Contemplating.

After a short pause, the woman speaks, finally raising her head.

"With... all due respect, sire… It's been six years since this search began. We know the demon must feast frequently to survive, but there's been virtually no increase in disappearances or mysterious deaths across the cities. Isn't it possible the demon has left the kingdom?"

Rodin's eyes snap open. His gaze locks onto hers, burning with barely-restrained fury.

The woman immediately lowers her head again, her expression unchanging, though her ears flatten the slightest bit against her skull.

"...Forgive me, Your Majesty."

Rodin regains his composure, taking a deep breath.

"Listen, Solanna... Demons are cunning. Insatiable. They don't just 'leave' an area they've already infested. They're **parasites**—they hunt and feast, leeching off the lifeblood of a thriving city, no matter what it takes... The war taught us as much," he says, leaning back against his throne.

"They know how to bide their time… How to make others wonder if they ever existed at all, before striking when it's least expected."

He rises from his throne, staring down at Solanna as sunlight streams through the massive stained glass window behind him.

"I will **not** be the king who sits idly by while a demon lurks among his people, waiting to _feast_ upon their _**souls**_," he declares, his words spilling out with chilling finality.

Solanna wants to object. She's unsure.

Unnerved.

_Unconvinced._

But she would rather not risk stoking the flames of Rodin's clearly displeased soul.

"Of course, Your Majesty."

Silence falls over the hall.

"...One last question… if I may."

"Go on…" Rodin says, raising an eyebrow.

Solanna lifts her head once more.

"Gumera Cavern was sealed off centuries ago, during the war. Why the sudden decision to reopen it? By all accounts, it's nothing but a large, empty space. Nothing of worth... no valuable ore... nothing more than a home to ravenous beasts."

Rodin sits back down, his eyes closing with a thoughtful expression before he answers.

"Gumera Cavern became an underground stronghold for demons during the war. It wasn't impenetrable by any means, but they'd somehow taken control of the creatures within, using them as minions."

He recounts the event as though he'd been there himself.

"The battle to take the cavern ended in victory. Brutal... but a victory nonetheless. Once the demons were eradicated, we sealed the cavern, trapping any remaining creatures down there and ensuring the enemy couldn't use it as a base again."

He opens his eyes, leaning forward slightly.

"The cavern entrance sits near a path merchants often travel to reach Ravenhallow. Recently, we've gotten reports of _sounds_ coming from it. Strange, isn't it? There are multiple layers of hard rock, several inches thick, all fused to the cavern walls. Sound shouldn't be able to escape at all."

He takes a deep breath and crosses his arms.

"Based on this, I've decided to have the cave unsealed and cleared out for good. Better to eliminate the problem before it becomes a catastrophe."

Solanna's eyes widen slightly with realization.

"Ah... So _that's_ why you sent Aisha and my sister with them?"

"Precisely," Rodin says with a light nod. "Aisha's raw power and Leilanna's ruthless flames are more than enough to exterminate whatever beasts remain within. I would have sent _you_ along with your sister instead, but... I still need one of you here to look after Lysandra."

Solanna's thoughts drift to the two knights. Leilanna and Aisha are one-woman armies in their own right. Leilanna more so than Aisha—that girl is still relatively inexperienced—but there's no doubt the two make an absurdly powerful duo... even despite their frequent disagreements.

"I see. Very well, then. I shall return to my post," she says.

Rodin nods. Solanna stands, turning and walking back the way she came. The double doors shut behind her as she exits.

Rodin maintains his stoicism for only a moment longer before lowering his head, his brow furrowing as he grits his teeth and clenches his fists. He brings one hand up to grab his head, an angry, almost desperate look crossing his face.

"Damn it..." he hisses quietly.

### Meanwhile, near Ravenhallow...

Hikari lies in a void blacker than the empty space between stars. Nothing surrounds him but the sound of dripping water and a low, rumbling ambiance.

Soon enough, he wakes.

Already bewildered, he sits up. He can't see anything—not his hands, not the ground, nothing. His eyes start to ache as they strain, desperate to take in any scrap of light.

Where is he? Why is it so dark? Why is it so _cold?_

The only thing he remembers is his study session. Everything after that is fuzzy. His thoughts race as he struggles to get his bearings. There's a cold wetness against his back—he's been lying in a shallow puddle.

He looks around. Or rather, _tries_ to.

The darkness is suffocating. Perhaps it's his fear kicking in, but each breath feels like a genuine struggle to get air into his lungs. It's as if the darkness isn't only suppressing any trace of light, but the very **atmosphere** within it.

His eyes still aching, he reaches out blindly, trying to find something—anything—to grab onto.

His fingers brush against something hard, weirdly smooth, and moist. A rock, perhaps?

He gathers the courage to call out.

_"H-Hello?!"_

...

No answer. His voice reverberates loudly throughout the space, making his ears ring.

A cave. He's in a dark, dank cave.

This must be some kind of nightmare. The atmosphere alone is too unsettling to be real.

Then...

He hears the hollow whistle of wind.

A breeze blows past him.

The cave trembles.

Hikari freezes. Adrenaline floods his system all at once. He can't bring himself to turn around. After trying his hardest to steel himself—even slightly—he slowly turns his head back toward the source of the wind, straining to see something, _anything_, in the blackness.

His blood runs cold.

The darkness is growling at him.

### Just outside the cavern...

The expedition team approaches the entrance to Gumera Cavern, taking note of the overgrowth around its exterior.

One woman at the front of the team—a woman with short auburn hair, cat ears atop her head, and a perpetually irritated glare—steps forward.

Leilanna.

She has a dagger holstered on both hips, and her tail flicks with sharp, agitated movements. Her eyes scan the rock blocking the entrance for a moment before she crosses her arms and steps back, gesturing to a few of the Earth mages in the team.

"Alright. Hurry up and get to work," she commands.

Three of the mages nod and step forward, arranging themselves in a triangular formation around the entrance. In one smooth motion, they thrust their arms forward, hands opening with palms pointed at the rock. An ethereal, glowing light flares from their hands.

A faint rumbling soon emanates from the entrance. The earth beneath the team starts to quake. A young woman stands just a few feet to Leilanna's left, wearing a green cowl and light armor, black rabbit ears perched atop her head. One ear twitches as she munches on a delectable fruit & nut bar, humming to herself.

Aisha.

She's the first to feel the ground shake beneath her.

"Whoa…" she utters in awe, watching the rock start to shift.

The ground continues to tremble. After mere seconds, the first rock layer cracks. It recedes into the walls of the cavern entrance, its movements rigid and unnatural as it melds back into the earth.

The second layer recedes faster. The entrance expands as more rock peels away.

Aisha puts a hand on her hip, furrowing her brow and letting out a light sigh.

"Man… what I would give to be able to do something like that…" she mutters enviously under her breath.

Leilanna's sensitive ears catch every word. She shoots a glance at Aisha, raising an eyebrow and narrowing her eyes.

"Is it not enough that you could obliterate all this rock with your own two feet?"

Aisha looks at Leilanna, unsurprised that she was heard. She merely shrugs, lightly tapping the ground with her foot.

"Well, sure, but… it'd be nice to have a break from getting physical for a change. My legs _do_ get tired, you know." Her response comes out a bit too casual for Leilanna's liking.

"Right…" Leilanna turns her gaze back to the shifting rock, barely concealing her exasperation.

_How many times has Aisha envied others? How many times has she neglected to appreciate her own strength? How many times has she acted so carefree? Why does it seem to bother no one else but_ _**me?**_

The thoughts race through Leilanna's head before she forces them away, not wanting to distract herself from the task at hand.

The mages continue their masterful work. Each rock layer cracks and recedes one after the other, until they finally expose a cluster of tightly packed boulders—the next obstacle. The cavern's mouth has opened wider now.

The moment the boulders are exposed, Aisha's ears twitch. Her eyes widen the slightest bit as she freezes, turning her head a few degrees to the right and angling her left ear toward the cavern.

Leilanna notices the shift in her posture and glances over.

"Something wrong?" Suspicion crawls up her spine as she instinctively moves her hands close to her daggers.

Aisha says nothing. As one boulder breaks apart, and then another, her ears twitch again. She's hearing something from deep, deep within the cavern.

Screaming.

She gasps.

"There's someone down there!" she yells, her voice sharp with urgency.

L'eiana's eyes widen in shock. "What?!"

Aisha whirls toward one of the expedition members behind her. "I'm going in! Give me a light stone, **NOW!**"

The sudden authority in Aisha's voice startles the man. He fumbles with his satchel before pulling out a small white stone and tossing it to her. She catches it and bolts toward the cavern's entrance, the Earth mages stumbling out of her way.

Leilanna steps forward, equally caught off guard by Aisha's sudden urgency.

"Hold on! Aisha, are you sure?! What did you hear?!"

Aisha raises her leg, muscles tensing, tendons tightening like coiled springs. She glances back at L'eiana, eyes gleaming with determination and, perhaps, a little arrogance. "Damn it, you have ears, don't you?! OPEN THEM!"

Leilanna recoils, one eye twitching. The _**nerve**_ of this girl!

"Mind your tongue, you DISRESPECTFUL **LITTLE—!!**"

Aisha's heel slams into the mass of stone as she axe-kicks it hard. The boom cuts Leilanna's reprimand short. In an instant, most of the rock caves inward, spiderwebbing with cracks. Aisha kicks it again. This time, it shatters. The entire cavern trembles as chunks of stone cascade into the larger space below.

A wave of pain shoots up her leg. She doesn't usually kick anything with this kind of force, let alone a massive chunk of stone. Still, it barely fazes her. She sprints into the dark cavern, eyes still gleaming with that determined shine.

The stone in her hand flares with bright light, cutting through the darkness effortlessly as she descends deeper and deeper.

Leilanna huffs with frustration, straightening before turning to the expedition team.

"Alright, change of plans! Into the cavern! Hurry up!"

She runs into the abyss, the team and Earth mages following close behind. What was supposed to be an extermination operation has turned into a search-and-rescue. At the very least... they're prepared.

For what's waiting down there—something ancient and very, very hungry—they have to be.
