### July 18th, 20XX

### Tokyo, Japan

**18 hours.**

Hikari, a brilliant student, has been studying for 18 hours straight.

_"It's nothing,"_ he thinks.

_"I've done this a hundred times before."_

And he has.

For months, he's worked _tirelessly_, pushing himself to the limit, focusing on one goal: getting into a top university. Nothing else matters.

Nothing else **can** matter.

His friends and family have called, knocked—all wearing the same expressions of concern. But Hikari has brushed them aside, unconcerned with his own health. He'll be fine. He's _**always**_ been fine.

Except…

"...central Tokyo area continues to experience record-breaking heat. The temperature at noon today reached 40.8 degrees Celsius, setting a new all-time high for…"

The radio drones on.

A heatwave. On the day before entrance exams, no less.

In his tiny, poorly ventilated apartment, Hikari feels like he's inside an oven. Each breath he takes feels like he’s inhaling fire. But he's tuned it out—tuned it _all_ out—his focus locked on something he’s deemed far more important.

**Studying.**

As the sun sets, he pauses. A splitting headache slams into him. He grabs his head with one hand. The heart palpitations and blurry vision were annoying enough. And now this?

He decides to end this final cram session.

His skin, flushed an unnatural, angry red, radiates with heat. His chest burns. His rapid breathing only worsens the feeling of fire in his throat. His eyes, bloodshot and dry, can no longer produce tears.

He stands. But the moment he takes a step, he freezes. The burning in his chest crescendos into a sharp, stabbing pain. His vision blurs as he falls, a loud ringing filling his ears. He reaches out weakly, grabbing the edge of his desk and pulling papers down with him as heat stroke claims him. He hits the ground with a loud _thud._
