## Overview

Magic, as it's understood in this world, is a force of of universe much like physics or gravity. It has no tangible form; it merely exists everywhen and everywhere, and can be drawn upon by those with mana to conjure and/or manipulate certain elements and forces.

Magic is inherently a reality-warping force. By drawing upon that force using mana, any individual can become a reality-bender. However, this comes with obvious caveats:

- No one can simply wave their hand and create a black hole. Not only would such a thing require an absurd mana capacity that no individual would ever be able to achieve in their lifetime, it would require an equally absurd amount of skill and mana control.
- Various magic techniques, such as healing magic, require knowledge in specific fields in order to be used effectively.
- The more complex a spell, the more mana it requires. A spell's complexity mostly just depends on what it's trying to do and its area of effect (if any). Levitating a pebble, for example, is very basic and requires very little mana. Something like creating a large ring of fire on the ground, on the other hand, requires a more substantial amount.

Magic, like all learned subjects, is taught in various schools and academies across the planet. _Thaumology_ is the name for the study of magic.

Magic usage comes down to two main techniques: _conjuration_ and _bending._

## Healing Magic?

Healing magic in this world is **entirely different** from the common interpretations of it in other fantasy media. Not only is healing magic considered the most difficult field of magic to master, it is highly illegal to even _use_ healing magic if you're not a doctor or at least _some_ form of medical practitioner.

Technically, there is no such thing as _healing magic_. It is basically just warping reality via magic to repair injuries. But like with every other field of magic, the classification of "healing magic" still exists for the sake of simplicity.

Depending on the region, medical schools may include _thaumology_ in their curriculums alongside the usual anatomy, physiology, pathology, etc. Thaumology in this case refers not to the study of magic as a whole, but the study of magic's effects on the body when used for regenerative purposes, the ethics of it, and the various benefits and risks.  Using healing magic effectively requires _deep and extensive knowledge_ in human/demi-human biology. When you use magic to repair an injury, you _must_ know _**exactly**_ what you're healing, otherwise you risk worsening the injury. A healer must be aware of the nerves, blood vessels, muscle fibers, bones, layers of the dermis, and must be precise with their magic so as to not accidentally heal an adjacent structure that wasn't the intended target. Various steps must be taken before one even thinks about repairing an injury with healing magic, most notably:

- The wound must be cleaned properly. Healing a wound without cleaning it can and oftentimes _will_ trap dirt and debris in the body, which can easily lead to sepsis or other severe complications.
- The patient must be administered _some_ sort of anesthetic. This isn't necessary for minor injuries such as small scrapes or cuts, but for a larger wound, it becomes a major ethics issue if a patient is healed via magic without first ensuring that the area is numbed to at least some degree. The process of healing via magic, when done by a professional, is very slow and careful to ensure minimum risk of complications. Nerves, muscle fibers, and other tissues are constantly shifting and moving back into place while healing, and thus the process is extraordinarily painful when performed without anesthetizing the patient. Historically, the only time it's ever been appropriate to heal a patient without anesthetic was during war times when medics would heal soldiers on the battlefield. The reasons are fairly self-explanatory.
