Various magical afflictions can occur through various means. They all involve the [[Manachondria]] in one's body becoming impaired in some way.

---

## Overview

Magic Illnesses are any diseases/afflictions that affect the manachondria and the body's natural mana production. There have been many magic illnesses throughout history, including plagues that ravished populations centuries ago.

---

## Delayed-Onset Mana Disorder (DOMD)

**Delayed-Onset Mana Disorder**, otherwise known as DOMD, is a normally benign affliction that occurs when an individual uses their mana recklessly, either by burning through a massive amount of mana in a short time or straining their own manachondria by attempting to use magic when extremely low on or out of mana. The disorder manifests between 8-36 hours after the reckless mana usage.

DOMD is characterized by a sudden inability to control one's magic, even after mana is replenished. Trying to levitate an object while suffering DOMD will either result in nothing happening, or your magic flaring wildly and having unpredictable effects. Essentially, your magic becomes entirely unreliable and unpredictable due to your strained manachondria.

DOMD resolves itself. The affected individual merely needs to abstain from all magic usage for a few days and give the manachondria time to recover. However, continuing to use magic even when your manachondria are already strained can lead to the affliction worsening, either by extending the recovery time or by crippling the individual entirely. The manachondria can rupture and die from continuous strain, effectively destroying the very thing that allows you to use magic. If enough of them rupture, the individual can end up permanently and severely weakening their own magic, or just losing any and all ability to use magic at all.

DOMD is _not_ to be confused with **dysthaumia,** which is difficulty using your magic. DOMD is a sudden, abrupt inability to control your magic, with unpredictable effects when magic use is attempted. Control still exists with dysthaumia, but for one reason or another, conjuration/bending is more difficult than it should be.

## Potion Sickness

Potion Sickness is an affliction that mainly occurs in two ways:

- Drinking a mana restoration potion, and then drinking another one within a very short time frame from the first.
- Drinking a mana restoration potion with a concentration and strength too high for your biology to handle.

When one drinks a mana restoration potion, they **must** wait at least two hours before attempting to drink another. This is the _minimum_ amount of time required for the manachondria to stabilize after the surge in mana production caused by the first potion. Drinking another potion too soon after the first is _not_ usually lethal, but the consequences are still unpleasant. The body, already in the middle of a mana surge, will reject the second potion outright. Some absorption of the second potion is inevitable, however, and these altogether will lead to:

- Vomiting (it will often sparkle and glow blue; this is the second potion that was expelled)
- Migraine-like headaches
- Weakness
- Temporary **athaumia** (loss of magic control)
- Stomach pain lasting up to an hour
- Diarrhea

The second case, drinking a mana restoration potion of a strength too high for your biology, carries far greater risks. A Felinox who drinks a mana restoration potion meant for human consumption can soon find themselves severely debilitated. Oftentimes, it's a medical emergency that requires an immediate visit to the hospital. The reason for this is that in these cases, the body does not reject the consumed potion. Now, the act of drinking the potion itself is not what causes harm. For a few minutes, the mana surge happens as normal, but the demi-human's body soon becomes overloaded because the manachondria are producing mana at an extreme rate that only a human would be able to handle. **This**\* is the danger. Drinking a potion far beyond the strength you can handle can, and often does, lead to [[Magic Illnesses#Mana Oversaturation|fatal Mana Oversaturation.]] Because of how fast mana restoration potions act, the victim will need emergency medical attention within _no less_ than 30 minutes in most cases. Chances of survival decrease drastically from that point.

## Mana Oversaturation

Mana Oversaturation is a surprisingly common condition that can be caused by various different factors. Most commonly, it's caused by something going wrong with kidney function. More often than not, mana oversaturation is a red flag; a sign of a larger, underlying problem. No direct act, with the exception of drinking a mana potion that's too strong for you, can cause mana oversaturation.

Mana oversaturation happens when excess mana levels within the body get too high. Since the kidneys are the sole organs responsible for filtering excess mana out of the body, any damage to them will affect how much excess mana is in your body. Kidney impairment/disease is often _identified_ through abnormally high excess mana readings in blood test results. Since excess mana accumulates in the bloodstream, symptoms are usually widespread throughout the body.

While mana oversaturation _can_ happen as a result of the manachondria producing mana far too fast for the body to keep up with rather than just the kidneys not working properly, this is very uncommon. There are no known diseases or natural conditions that would cause mana production to surge in such a way. Such a condition is usually the result of a birth defect.

Mana Oversaturation is not immediately dangerous; there _are_ different stages to it, but it _can_ be fatal if left unchecked and excess mana climbs to lethal levels.

### Mild Oversaturation

Mild mana oversaturation is the first stage of excess mana accumulation in the bloodstream. It's actually very common in young kids starting/going through puberty, as the manachondria (like the rest of the body) experience explosive growth in numbers and efficiency, beginning to produce mana at an adult level speed. The symptoms, while harmless, can still be inconvenient:

- A faint "buzzing" feeling in the body
- Minor **thaumorrhea** (involuntary magic discharge; uncontrollable, but infrequent and not dangerous. It's just the body attempting to expel some of the excess mana through forced magic usage)
- Light, dull body aches

### Moderate Oversaturation

Moderate mana oversaturation is where things start to get more concerning, at least depending on the individual and their lifestyle. It's common among warriors and knights who are frequently sparring/fighting, and can potentially suffer blows to the kidneys that damage them. Usually, the mana oversaturation is not too dangerous for these individuals. Their frequent magic usage will still balance out the levels of mana in their bodies, preventing any real symptoms. That doesn't mean their kidneys don't still need to get looked at by a doctor, though.

Outside of military contexts however, moderate oversaturation is almost always the first sign of kidney function impairment. Moderate oversaturation is not normally fatal, but can be debilitating, especially for children and the elderly. Symptoms include:

- A more prominent buzzing feeling
- More frequent thaumorrhea capable of causing injury to others
- Sharper body aches
- Fever
- Headaches
- Weakness
- Fatigue
- Visible, faintly glowing veins

### Severe Oversaturation

Reaching severe mana oversaturation means that something has gone _horribly_ wrong. Either your kidneys have failed entirely, or you drank a very strong mana potion when you needed a weaker one. Severe oversaturation _can_ be fatal if the victim isn't given _immediate_ medical attention. When excess mana accumulates to this level, the blood (for lack of a better term) begins to _boil._ Not in the temperature sense. The intense concentration of mana in the bloodstream starts to destroy red blood cells, damage blood vessel walls, and cause dangerously high blood pressure that can rupture blood vessels, all of which is characterized by a "burning" feeling throughout the body (distinct from fever).  Aside from this, the symptoms include everything previously stated, but on a much worse scale, along with:

- Brightly glowing veins
- Brightly glowing eyes, coupled with impaired vision
- Auditory and visual hallucinations
- Delirium
- Seizures, coupled with severe, dangerous thaumorrhea (the body desperately attempting to expel the excess mana)
- If the body's attempts to expel the excess mana fail, _**death**_

Essentially, the body tears itself apart from the inside out. It's a horrific thing to witness. The survival rate of severe oversaturation is actually fairly high due to just how much mana the body expels in its attempts to save itself; death is more common in cases of mana potion consumption (since the manachondria are being stimulated to produce large amounts of mana far beyond the body's ability to filter it out or expel it), but those who survive almost always suffer permanent disabilities such as brain damage.
