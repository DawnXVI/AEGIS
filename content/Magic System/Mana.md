The energy that facilitates the use of  magic. No spell can be reliably cast without it.

---

## Overview

Mana is a natural energy required to harness the power of magic. It is produced and stored within the [[Manachondria|manachondria]]. Any usage of magic will expend mana, and the mana drain itself depends on what exactly you're trying to do. Conjuring a faint spark to light a candle? Simple, easy; low mana cost. Repairing a severe, gaping wound that would otherwise require emergency surgery? Dangerous, incredibly difficult, requires the utmost precision and concentration; high mana cost.

All individuals possess some level of mana, even those of races with extremely low [[Manachondria|manachondria]] counts.

Mana is a **"magical stamina."** Everyone starts off with a low mana capacity at birth, no matter how low their [[Manachondria|manachondria]] count may be. Mana capacity typically increases naturally and gradually as you reach adulthood, though it can be increased through training and practice.

Mana functions a lot like a muscle. Through regular "exercise," one can increase their mana capacity, improve their magic control, and improve the strength of their magic. However, unlike muscles, there seems to be no limit to how far one can increase their mana. Thus, it is theoretically possible to achieve virtually **infinite** levels of mana, though such a feat would require you to live centuries upon centuries to feasibly achieve it.

The bodies of all humans as well as all demi-humans who have magic capability are very well-adapted for mana production and all it entails. Most notably, excess mana often builds up in the body and bloodstream due to the manachondria producing mana when one's already full on it. This is entirely normal, and it's common for small amounts of excess mana to show up on blood test results. Excess mana is filtered through the kidneys and expelled via urination.

## Depletion

Every magic user has depleted their mana at least once in their life. For a rookie practitioner, it's a common state they may find themselves in after conjuring one too many fireballs during practice. For a professional mage, depleting your mana is something you only do if you're a reckless buffoon, or if you have no other choice.

Mana, contrary to what you might believe, is **not** entirely required to use magic. Mana doesn't _bring_ magic into existence; it stabilizes it, facilitates the process that gives it a tangible form, and strengthens it to a level at which it has practical use. Mana exists as a natural resource produced by the body because evolution deemed it a safe and convenient intermediate to power the processes that make magic usable.

Still, mana has a limit, and when this limit is reached, various effects can occur, [[Magic Illnesses|some more detrimental than others.]]

When low on mana, magic capabilities immediately become limited. Though mana is not required for physical exertion, being low on mana will still cause mental and even physical fatigue. Attempting to use magic at any complex level while low on mana can cause it to become unstable, and it can even **backfire** on the user if they aren't careful.

When mana is fully depleted, you are essentially totally defenseless against any magical attacks. You cannot use magic without it becoming unstable, and even the smallest, weakest fireball **will** scar you. Mental and physical fatigue begin  to take their full toll as well when mana is depleted. Poor focus, brain fog, headaches, exhaustion, and body aches are common. Straining the body any further **will** have severe, adverse effects.

## Replenishing Mana

Mana is a self-replenishing resource. The body and [[Manachondria|manachondria]] are constantly working to refill mana, and the speed of this process depends on race (because of biological differences in manachondria count).

Mana replenishes steadily over time as the manachondria process residual mana in the air and do their work. For most humans, the average time it takes for the body to naturally replenish its mana to full from empty is around 4-5 hours. This is, by the standards of the rest of the world, extremely fast, but it's not surprising given humans' natural magic superiority and high average manachondria counts.

Eating food and sleeping will both speed up the process of mana production. The manachondria, like everything else in the body, requires nourishment to remain functional, and eating a good meal after a training session will quickly re-energize your manachondria. Sleep puts the body in a highly active state of restoration, and that restoration includes the manachondria. With more energy allocated to the manachondria, they're able to very quickly produce more mana, ensuring a full supply after a good night's rest.

Mana can also be restored via the consumption of _potions and elixirs._ There are shops run by professional alchemists who concoct various tonics and useful substances, as well as mana restoration potions using natural and enchanted ingredients. What these potions do is stimulate the manachondria into an extremely active state of rapid mana production. Once the potions take effect, mana supply begins increasing very fast as the manachondria produce mana on overdrive mode. One who consumes a mana restoration potion can expect to be back to full arcane strength within minutes. However, there are caveats to this. Any potential consumer of mana restoration potions must be medically cleared before they can consume or even _buy_ any. Mana restoration potions are, essentially, drugs. Stimulants. They create drastic changes in the body that can be dangerous for those who aren't biologically equipped to handle it. Mana restoration potions exist in many different varieties and formulas depending on one's race, as every race's biology, is different. A Felinox who wants to buy a mana potion will need one that's much lower in strength than what a human would consume.

## Mana Barriers

Mana Barriers are essentially thin, invisible forcefields around the body that hover _just_ over the skin. Mana barriers are what prevent individuals from being harmed by their own magic. They are what allows someone to conjure a fireball in their hand and not be burnt despite the extremely close proximity.

The body draws upon its mana supply to maintain the mana barrier. Because of this, the mana barrier fades and can even disappear entirely when low or empty on mana. It will of course come back once the individual's mana has replenished by a sufficient amount.

Mana barriers also act as a shield, or more accurately a _filter_, against other peoples' magic, though the strength of one's mana barrier is dictated by the strength of their magic. Stronger magic means a stronger mana barrier. Mana barriers act as filters because they control how much magic actually gets through when one is hit by a magic attack. A novice mage's mana barrier would do very little to filter the magic that comes through when they are hit by a fireball cast by a professional sorcerer. At best, only a minuscule amount of the fireball's magic potency would be nullified. The mage would still be severely burnt. Conversely, the professional sorcerer' mana barrier would be strong enough that a fireball cast by the novice mage would be almost entirely snuffed out. At best, the sorcerer would feel only a small bit of warmth where the fireball hits him.

The filtering ability of mana barriers means that a magic attack _never_ hits at its _true, full strength._ No matter how weak the target's mana barrier is, there will _always_ be some amount of the magic that is nullified. The sole exception is when the target lacks a mana barrier entirely.

Having no mana barrier (either because you have depleted all of your mana or you are a Lapin) makes you extremely susceptible to magic. The smallest fireball would be able to scorch you, and your own magic would be able to hurt you. This is why it's dangerous to use magic when you're out of mana; your mana barrier fades and cannot protect you from your own magic.
