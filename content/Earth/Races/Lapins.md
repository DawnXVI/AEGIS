#### Blessed with Unparalleled Strength

## Overview

Lapins were a demi-human race of, essentially, rabbit people. They were peaceful, prideful, and powerful warriors who for countless centuries lived in peace and prosperity in [[The Great Lapin City]], which many scholars still agree today was the closest thing to a _true utopia_ the world had ever seen.

---

## Physical Characteristics

### Hair

Lapins often have multi-colored hair. Hair colors range from white, black, and varying shades of brown. Solid single-color hair is a recessive trait, and thus is fairly rare.

Both male and female Lapins most commonly have short hair that almost never goes beyond neck-length. Long hair is exceedingly uncommon among Lapins both due to genetics and practicality. As warriors, they can't allow their hair to grow out very long, as it would become a major hindrance in combat. This persistence shaped the gene pool over time. Lapins with a genetic predisposition towards longer hair became progressively rarer until the trait had largely faded from the population.

### Ears & Tail

Lapins are defined by the tall rabbit ears that rest on their heads and the round tails they have on their lower backs. While this is in itself unremarkable, as there are many other races with the ears and tail of an animal, Lapins are iconic in a way that, in the case of humans, any time someone mentions the term "demi-human", a _Lapin_ is the first thing that pops into most people's minds.

Ear and tail fluffiness varies between Lapins, but the most consistent thing about them is that their fur color is always the same as the color of the Lapin's hair. Rarely, however, genetic mutations can cause hair and ear/tail colors to be completely different from each other. Historically, Lapins born with mutations such as these were seen as bad omens who would bring misfortune upon their families. This changed after the Lapins, and the rest of the world, bore witness to the incredible feats performed by **Edith Haddon**, the strongest Lapin in recorded history. By the 20th century, superstitions surrounding genetic mutations had largely evaporated.

### Strength

Lapins were long renowned for their immense strength. To fight a Lapin in hand-to-hand combat was to set yourself up for quick and inevitable defeat.

## Biology

### Manachondria

Lapins have **virtually no [[Manachondria|manachondria]] to speak of**. While their bodies are adapted to process residual mana, the presence of manachondria in their cells is incredibly minuscule. Thus, Lapins cannot naturally produce mana (at least not in any meaningful amounts), and as a result, they're _incapable of using magic._

This lack of magical ability is a direct trade-off for their immense strength. Over the course of history, Lapins have only ever had their strength to rely on. They evolved to have superior physical prowess, and with their strength, there was never any need for magic.

### Muscle

Lapins have incredibly dense muscles that are optimized for explosive power. Their legs are capable of generating 3-4x more force than their arms. From childhood, Lapins are already physically stronger than most children of other races. This strength increases rapidly through ages 11-14 as their muscle develops further.

An untrained adolescent Lapin can lift around 300 lbs (136 kg) with their arms. There is no measurement for untrained adult Lapin lifting strength simply because there are no untrained adult Lapins.

A trained Lapin who has only completed the mandatory 2-year combat training is capable of lifting upwards of 2,000 lbs (907 kg). Those who remain in combat training and seek further improvement or even military service will often far exceed this benchmark.

### Skeleton

Lapin bones are incredibly dense; _far_ denser than other races. This is a biological requirement that ensures their skeletons can withstand the force their muscles generate, and the impact of striking other objects. Their joints are reinforced with more cartilage and connective tissue, enough to sufficiently absorb the impact of their high-speed movements and kicks.

### Heart & Lungs

Lapins have substantially larger hearts than most people. They pump blood with incredible force and speed, which means they also have very thick ventricular walls in order to withstand their own blood pumping strength and avoid rupturing, especially with how fast their hearts can beat. What would be a panicked heart rate (90-120 bpm) for most races is a resting heart rate for Lapins, and what would normally cause quick cardiac failure (200-250+ bpm) for most is merely the average heart rate of a Lapin in active combat (or engaging in any other strenuous physical activity).

Going to a smaller scale, Lapin blood has a higher red blood cell count, which allows for incredibly efficient oxygen delivery. Their blood vessels are also thicker and more elastic, which allows them to withstand what would otherwise be life-threateningly high blood pressure.

Lapins are built to be able to meet the absurd oxygen demands of their bodies. Most notably, they have larger lung capacity, and their lungs themselves have more efficient gas exchange. Even at rest, Lapins are often breathing more rapidly than others.

### Adrenaline

**The most well-known Lapin trait among biologists.** Lapins have adrenal systems that produce _significantly_ more adrenaline than any other race. They have _hair-trigger_ fight-or-flight responses, and this adrenal hyperactivity provides several advantages, namely: extreme reaction speeds rivaling those of Felidaes/Felinoxes, and virtual immunity to pain during combat. Strong emotions such as rage also trigger massive adrenaline surges that can push a Lapin's physical capabilities to terrifying heights (though at risk of injury to their own bodies).

Such a hyperactive adrenal system of course also comes with some debilitating weaknesses. Lapins **have poor endurance** in comparison to all races, and are _not_ built for prolonged combat. They're warriors who _end_ fights as quickly as they start. Physical symptoms like exhaustion, nausea, and trembling are common after combat. The painkiller effect of their adrenaline also rapidly fades, and so any injuries they sustained during combat will make themselves known within seconds.

These weaknesses are heavily focused on during the 2-year combat training, with great effort placed in training to mitigate them as much as possible, though there is only so much one can do to mitigate weaknesses such as these. One thing trained Lapins are incredibly good at, however, is turning fear into power.

Normally, a hair-trigger stress response like the one Lapins have would mean they are very easily frightened and made to flee or freeze in place out of fear. This is absolutely true for Lapin _children,_ but _not_ for grown Lapins who have already gone through combat training. During training, Lapins aren't taught to _mitigate_ their stress response, but instea&#x64;_&#x75;se_ it; _convert_ it to enhanced physical performance. They're taught to **always** choose "fight" rather than "flight", to always _react instantly_ rather than _freeze in place_. It's similar to how, in real life, a soldier in the military would be trained to remain calm and focused under extreme pressure.

### Metabolism

As if it wasn't already obvious, the Lapin biological engine runs _**incredibly**_ hot. Their body's individual systems and processes are insanely demanding, and this means they require large amounts of food just to keep themselves running at baseline efficiency. Lapins will often consume 2-3x more food per day than other races of similar size. Missing any meals will rapidly degrade performance. Such an intense nutritional need has greatly shaped Lapin culture around food.

### Lifespan

Inevitably, the stars that burn brightest burn out the fastest. Lapins have the shortest lifespan of any race at just _50-60 years_ on average. Reaching old age was considered a significant achievement.

## Culture

### Family

**The greatest core value of the Lapin race.** There is nothing more important to a Lapin than their loved ones. They see the word "family" as a very broad term that doesn't just apply to blood relatives. It can just as easily be applied to adopted relatives or very close friends.

Obviously, not many people would hesitate to protect their loved ones, but Lapins took this concept and cranked it up by 1,000. The _entire point_ of the mandatory combat training they all went through was to make them strong enough to protect themselves and their loved ones. Family, friends, and lovers always take priority over anything else in the eyes of a Lapin.

### Intimacy

It's no secret that Lapins wore their hearts on their sleeves. They had very little difficulty expressing how they feel and what exactly they wanted in the moment. Their culture was one that celebrated honesty and emotional connection. This resulted in healthy and often very happy relationships between Lapin lovers.

Communication, arguably the most important aspect of a healthy relationship, came very easy for Lapins. Feelings weren't hidden from one another, both partners would know where they stand with each other, and trust was overall very high. Public displays of affection were common for Lapin couples. Though some cultures saw this as strange, it was the casual approach to sex Lapins have that often made people outside of their culture raise an eyebrow, as both emotional AND sexual intimacy were very important to Lapins. They saw sex not just as an act of love, but also as one of the best ways to strengthen their bonds with their partners.

Lapins highly valued monogamy and were known to be extremely loyal and devoted partners. Divorce rates were near non-existent, as they took the concept of "til death do us part" very literally. Adding on to this, childhood friendships that blossomed into romantic relationships were extremely common. Most married Lapin couples knew each other since they were very young.
